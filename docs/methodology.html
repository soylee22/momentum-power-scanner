METHODOLOGY  |  MOMENTUM POWER

How the scanner finds the top 10.

A plain-English walkthrough of every step the scanner takes between "here are US and UK stocks above $1bn market cap" and "here are the ten strongest momentum names this week." No black boxes. No tuned parameters. Just three filters, three rankings, and one number that tells you who is leading.

01 / The big picture

Filter first, then rank.

The scanner splits the work into two phases. The first phase is a filter. Every stock either passes or fails based on eight strict tests. The second phase is a ranker. Among the survivors, three different momentum signals are averaged to produce one composite score.

The filter is binary by design. It does not weight things. It does not compromise. A stock either looks like it is in a sustained uptrend or it doesn't. This stops the scanner from rewarding stocks that are merely less bad than their peers in a falling market.

The ranker is what produces the leaderboard. Once you know a stock is genuinely in an uptrend, the question becomes: how strong is this uptrend compared to other uptrends right now? That is what the composite score measures.

PIPELINE

  1. Build the universe: Yahoo Finance screener for US + UK equities with market cap ≥ $1bn.
  2. Download 14 months of daily prices for each ticker.
  3. Compute relative strength and trend-template features for every stock.
  4. Apply the eight Minervini gates. Survivors continue, the rest are filtered out.
  5. Rank the survivors on three independent momentum signals.
  6. Take the top 10 by composite score and publish the digest, dashboard, and charts.
02 / The universe

Where the candidates come from.

The hard floor is $1bn market capitalisation. Below that, liquidity and gap risk get worse on a retail platform, and the Stage 2 template starts picking up thin names that do not behave like the mid/large-cap momentum book this scanner is built for.

Both legs come from the Yahoo Finance equity screener (via yfinance): region US on the main Nasdaq/NYSE/AMEX boards, region GB on the LSE, market cap ≥ $1bn. Preferreds, warrants, notes, and LSE international (digit-prefixed) lines are dropped. Yahoo’s own floor leaks a little at the tail, so the scanner re-checks market cap on every quote.

A small set of curated LSE alts (physical gold/silver, sector and factor ETFs) sits outside the cap filter so commodities and factor legs can still surface when they trend.

Returns are computed in local currency (USD for US stocks, GBP for UK stocks). FX moves are not part of momentum, they are noise that contaminates the signal. A British investor who held a US stock benefitted from FX too, but that is a separate question from "is this stock genuinely strong?".

03 / The filter

Mark Minervini's eight Stage 2 gates.

Mark Minervini is a US trader who won the US Investing Championship in 1997 and codified his approach in Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard. He divides every stock's life into four stages: Stage 1 (basing, going nowhere), Stage 2 (markup, going up), Stage 3 (topping, distribution), Stage 4 (markdown, going down).

You only want to own Stage 2 stocks. The other three stages are where money goes to die. Minervini's eight-gate trend template is his mechanical test for whether a stock is in Stage 2. If a stock fails any one of the eight tests, it is not Stage 2 and the scanner discards it.

Each gate is shown below with its plain-English meaning, the formal mathematical test, and what it actually filters out.

01

Price is above both the long-term averages.

price > 150-day SMA   AND   price > 200-day SMA

The simplest possible "is the trend up?" test. The 200-day moving average is the most-watched long-term trend line in the world. If the current price is below it, the stock is by definition in a downtrend on a long-term basis.

Filters out: stocks below their long-term trend, stocks recovering from a deep drawdown but not yet through resistance.

02

The medium-term average is above the long-term average.

150-day SMA > 200-day SMA

The 150-day average reacts faster than the 200-day. If the faster average is above the slower one, the trend is accelerating, not decaying. This is the moving-average equivalent of "recent momentum is stronger than older momentum".

Filters out: stocks where the medium-term trend has rolled over even though price might temporarily be above the 200-day.

03

The long-term average has been rising for at least a month.

200-day SMA today > 200-day SMA 21 trading days ago

A flat 200-day average is consistent with a stock going sideways. For a stock to be a real Stage 2 name, the 200-day average itself should be sloping upward. One month is the minimum; Minervini prefers four to five months.

Filters out: stocks where the 200-day average is flat or only just turning up. The trend has to be established, not nascent.

04

The averages are stacked in trend order.

50-day SMA > 150-day SMA   AND   50-day SMA > 200-day SMA

In a clean uptrend, you expect the shortest moving average to be highest, then medium, then longest. The 50-day average represents roughly the last two-and-a-half months of trading. If it's above both longer averages, the most recent quarter has been the strongest part of the trend.

Filters out: stocks where short-term action has weakened relative to the longer trend. Pullbacks. Distribution.

05

Price is above the short-term average too.

price > 50-day SMA

Even in a Stage 2 stock, price can dip below the 50-day during a normal correction. This gate insists the dip is over. The stock is back above its 50-day, suggesting buyers stepped in.

Filters out: stocks in active correction. We are buying breakouts, not catching falling knives.

06

Price is at least 30% above the 52-week low.

price >= 1.30 × 52-week low

A stock that just bounced off its low is not Stage 2 yet. It might be the start of one, but it might equally be a dead-cat bounce. Insisting on 30% above the low confirms a meaningful rally has already occurred, which is itself evidence the new trend is real.

Filters out: early bottom-fishers, stocks still close to their pain points.

07

Price is within 25% of the 52-week high.

price >= 0.75 × 52-week high

A stock that is more than 25% off its high is in correction, not Stage 2. The high might have been six months ago and the stock has been bleeding ever since. We want stocks that are still near their highs, not stocks that used to be high.

Filters out: faded former champions, stocks in extended consolidation more than a quarter off the high.

08

The relative strength rating is at least 70.

RS rating >= 70

All the previous gates are about the stock's own price history. This one is about how the stock compares to everything else. The relative strength rating ranks the stock against the entire universe; an RS of 70 means the stock has outperformed at least 70% of all stocks in the scan. The next section explains exactly how this is calculated.

Filters out: stocks that pass all the technical gates but are merely keeping pace with the market. Stage 2 demands relative leadership, not relative survival.

A stock has to clear all eight gates simultaneously. Failing any one of them is enough to drop the stock from the survivor pool, no matter how strong it looks on the others.

In a typical week roughly 15–25% of the universe will pass all eight. In a strong tape it can be higher. In a falling market it collapses towards zero, which is exactly the right behaviour: the scanner stops finding "winners" when there are none.

04 / The relative strength rating

How RS is computed.

The RS rating used here is the IBD-style construction (after the long-running US publication Investor's Business Daily). It is built in two steps: a weighted return is computed for each stock, and that weighted return is then ranked across the entire universe.

Step 1: the weighted return

For each stock the scanner takes its price return over four windows and averages them with declining weights:

weighted_return = 0.4 × r3m  +  0.2 × r6m  +  0.2 × r9m  +  0.2 × r12m

The most-recent quarter gets double the weight of any older quarter. Why? Because momentum decays. A stock that ripped twelve months ago and has stalled since is a different beast from a stock that ripped three months ago and is still ripping. The weighting rewards fresh strength, not stale strength.

Step 2: the percentile rank

The weighted return is then ranked across the full universe of ~2.5–3k stocks. A stock with a weighted return better than 95% of all other stocks gets an RS rating of 95. A median stock gets 50. The worst stocks get 1.

This is the same scaling IBD has been using since the 1980s. It strips out absolute return magnitudes (a +500% gain in a roaring tape might not be that special; a +30% gain in a flat tape can be world-class) and instead asks how unusual is this stock's strength compared to its peers right now?

05 / The composite score

How the top 10 is chosen.

Among the survivors of the eight gates, the scanner needs a way to decide which are strongest. One number is not enough, because momentum has different facets. So the composite combines three independent signals.

The three components

  1. RS rating. Recent-weighted return, ranked across the universe. Captures absolute strength relative to peers.
  2. 52-week-high proximity. The current price as a fraction of the 52-week high. Captures whether the stock is still in a clean uptrend or fading from a peak.
  3. 1-year total return. The simple "what did this stock do over the year" number, no weighting. Captures sustained, long-horizon strength.

The three components are on different scales (RS is 1–99, proximity is a percentage, 1-year return can be anywhere from −50% to several thousand percent). To combine them fairly, each is converted into a rank within the survivor pool: the best survivor on that signal gets 1.0, the worst gets 0.0, the median gets 0.5.

composite = ( rankRS  +  rankproximity  +  rank1y )  /  3

Equal weights. No tuning. The composite is then a number between 0 and 1. The top 10 stocks by composite are this week's leaderboard.

Why three signals not one? Because a stock can be strong on one measure and weak on another. A name with a brilliant 1-year return that has rolled over from its peak is structurally different from a name that is still pushing new highs. Averaging three independent ranks penalises one-trick performances and rewards stocks that look strong from every angle simultaneously.

06 / Worked example

How Intel scored 0.908 this week.

Take Intel. Among the 137 Stage 2 survivors this week, here is how its three component ranks added up:

SIGNAL RAW VALUE RANK WITHIN SURVIVORS
RS rating 98 ~ 0.95
Distance from 52-week high 0.0% ~ 0.85
1-year total return +457% ~ 0.93
Composite (mean of the three ranks) 0.908

Read the score as: "Intel is in roughly the top 10% of all Stage 2 survivors on every signal we measure, simultaneously."

Note that the proximity rank (0.85, not 1.0) reflects something important about this week. About 53 of the 137 survivors are tied at 0.0% distance from their 52-week high, so they all share a percentile rank around 0.85, the average rank of the tied positions. In a tape where fewer stocks are at fresh highs, this number would discriminate more.

07 / What the score does not tell you

Honest limits.

The scanner is a momentum tool. It is silent on three big things, and you should be silent about them too when you use it.

  1. It does not say if a stock is overvalued. There are no fundamentals in the model. Earnings, valuation, debt, management quality — all absent. A stock with a composite of 0.95 might be trading at 200x earnings.
  2. It does not predict future returns. Momentum is a backward-looking signal that tends to persist, not a guarantee that it will. Past Stage 2 stocks have rolled over without warning more than once.
  3. It does not give you a sell signal. The scanner only screens for entries. Exit discipline is your responsibility: trail a stop, watch for breaks below the 50-day, watch the tape regime.
  4. It does not adjust for risk. Two stocks at composite 0.9 may have very different volatilities. The scanner is silent on position sizing.
08 / Cadence and outputs

What the scanner produces, and when.

The scanner runs every Sunday evening (21:23 UTC) on GitHub Actions. It then publishes:

  1. A Gmail digest with the new top 10, week-on-week entries and exits.
  2. A dashboard at soylee22.github.io/momentum-power-scanner.
  3. Per-ticker charts with the 50/150/200-day moving averages and 52-week range bands marked.
  4. A CSV and Markdown table for downstream analysis.
  5. A JSON snapshot appended to data/history/, for tracking how the leaderboard evolves over time.

The price cache (data/prices.parquet) is incremental: every run only downloads bars since the last run. The history file builds up week-by-week so over time you can ask questions like "what was the composite of stock X on date Y?".
